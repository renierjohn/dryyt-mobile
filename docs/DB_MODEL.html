Dryyt DB Model

The worker's D1 database after migration 0030: 15 tables, what each holds, and which roles read and write it (R read, W write, R/W both; Admin is the cpanel). Amounts are centavos; times are UTC unless noted.

Tables

Accounts

users

Customer R/WDriver R/WOwner R/WAdmin R/W
~106k rowscustomers + drivers + owners

Every account: customers, drivers and shop owners. Admin access is a flag, not a role.

  • idINTEGER PK
  • emailTEXT unique
  • password_hashTEXT
  • name / phoneTEXT
  • rolecustomer · driver · owner
  • push_tokenTEXT, Expo push
  • maxstoreINTEGER, stores an owner may run (default 1)
  • is_admin0/1, cpanel access
  • blocked0/1, 1 = can't sign in
  • created_atTEXT UTC

drivers

Driver R/WAdmin R
~5,000 rowsone per driver

A driver's live state: online for drop offs / pickups, busy, last location.

  • user_idPK → users
  • online / pickup_online0/1
  • busy0/1, on a job
  • group_idarea they serve
  • lat / lnglast fix
  • updated_atTEXT UTC

driver_data

Customer RDriver R/WOwner WAdmin R/W
~5,000 rowsone per driver

Driver verification and earnings. Photo keys point into the PROOFS R2 bucket.

  • user_idunique → users (drivers only, by trigger)
  • photo_face / photo_idR2 keys
  • face_checkpassed · skipped
  • noteslicense plate
  • vehicle_type1 bicycle · 2 motorcycle · 3 tricycle · 4 car
  • status0/1 verified (locks details)
  • earningscentavos, running total
Stores

stores

Customer RDriver ROwner R/WAdmin R/W
~2,000 rows1,000 owners × 2

Food and laundry shops, with map pin, branding, area and the app's fee for the store.

  • idINTEGER PK
  • name / addressTEXT
  • typefood · laundry
  • owner_id→ users
  • group_idarea → rates.group_id
  • lat / lngmap pin
  • store_logoURL
  • store_payment_gatewayJSON, up to 3 payment QR codes
  • fee_type1 flat · 2 percentage
  • fee_flatcentavos
  • fee_percentage% of sales

store_menu_items

Customer RDriver WOwner R/WAdmin R
~48k rows1,200 food stores × 40 items

A store's menu (food) with pricing, ordering and popularity.

  • store_id→ stores
  • name / categoryTEXT
  • pricecentavos
  • discount_percent0–100
  • image_urlURL
  • best_seller0/1
  • sort_orderINTEGER
  • popularityqty delivered

driver_blocks

Driver ROwner R/WAdmin R
~4,000 rows≈ 2 per store

Drivers a shop won't take drop offs from.

  • store_id→ stores
  • driver_id→ users
  • created_atTEXT UTC
Pricing

rates

Customer RAdmin R/W
~160 rows20 areas × 2 services × 4 vehicles

Delivery pricing per area, service and vehicle. Bookings price with the motorcycle rate.

  • group_idarea
  • typepickup · dropoff

Pickup

transactions_pickup

Customer R/WDriver R/WOwner R/WAdmin R
~3.6M rows / year10,000 a day × 365

Pickup orders (food or laundry): customer → store → driver delivers.

  • idINTEGER PK (was orders)
  • typepickup
  • customer_id / driver_id / store_id→ users / stores
  • status(laundry: awaiting_fee → to_pay →) awaiting_payment → payment_confirmed → pending → accepted → picked_up → delivered · cancelled
  • items_totalcentavos; laundry: the laundry fee set by the shop
  • road_kmREAL, null for typed address
  • delivery_feedriver's share
  • service_feeapp's cut
  • total_delivery_feecustomer pays
  • payment_ref / payment_photo_keyproof of payment
  • customer_lat/lng · driver_lat/lngmap pins
  • driver_paid_atowner paid driver

order_items

Customer R/WDriver ROwner RAdmin R
~9.1M rows / year≈ 2.5 lines per order

Lines of a pickup order, with name and price copied at order time.

  • order_id→ transactions_pickup
  • menu_item_id→ store_menu_items (null once deleted)
  • name / price / qtyas ordered

  • customer_lat/lng/address · shop_lat/lngtrip

  • delivery_fee / service_fee / total_delivery_feecentavos
  • laundry_feefrom the shop

  • amountcentavos

Subscription payments owners make to the app, recorded from the cpanel.

At this pace the database grows by about 2.98 GB a year, almost all from pickup orders, their lines and drop offs. That reaches D1's 10 GB cap in roughly 3 years. Before then, archive finished transactions older than a year (e.g. to R2 or Basin) or split them into a second database.

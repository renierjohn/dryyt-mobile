Cloudflare usage and budget

Expected monthly usage of every Cloudflare service the Dryyt worker uses, at three sizes, with the cost and a recommended budget for each.

  • Prices are from Cloudflare's pricing pages, checked 2026-10-20. Re-check them before relying on the totals.
  • Usage is estimated from how the app actually calls the worker: poll intervals, socket messages and writes per order, taken from the code. These are planning numbers, not measurements. Once the app is live, compare them with the Cloudflare dashboard (Workers & Pages → dryyt-worker → Metrics, plus D1 and R2 metrics) and adjust.

How the platform counts usage, which these estimates rely on:

  • Incoming WebSocket messages count as Durable Object requests at 20:1 (100 messages = 5 requests). Opening a socket is 1 request. Outgoing messages and protocol pings are free.
  • Hibernation: an idle Durable Object isn't billed for duration, so duration is roughly the time spent handling messages. The memory charged is always 128 MB, whatever the object actually uses.
  • D1 indexes: every write to an indexed column also writes one row per index.
  • The Free plan isn't enough even at small scale. It allows 100,000 Worker requests a day, and this app's polling alone goes past that. Stay on Workers Paid.

Messages turn into requests at 20:1. Driver location messages are the biggest share: each online driver sends a fix about every 5 s (50 × 8 h × 720 = 8.6 M a month at small scale). Order rooms add the driver's location while a pickup is on its way (about 15 min × 5 s) plus pings. Halving how often drivers send their location (see Ways to pay less) roughly halves the requests and keeps large-scale duration inside the included 400,000 GB-s.

Recommended budget

Budget each month for the expected total plus headroom for growth, launch spikes and estimation error. Add an alert below each line, so you hear about it before the bill does.

To set the alert, use Cloudflare dashboard → Notifications → Add → Usage Based Billing. Add one for Workers requests, Durable Objects and R2, each sized from the tables above.

Year 2 at large scale: add D1 storage (≈ $1–4 a month once past 5 GB) and the growing R2 storage (≈ $13 a month for each extra 880 GB a year), unless you archive old data (below).

Ways to pay less

Ordered by how much they save. Most of the bill at medium and large scale is polling (Workers requests) and driver location messages (Durable Objects). Savings are per month against the expected totals above, year-1 average.

#ChangeSmallMediumLarge
1Poll less on screens that already have a socket. Customer and owner screens poll every 5 s while focused. Pushing changes over the existing rooms and polling every 30–60 s only as a fallback cuts Workers requests and CPU time about in half.$0$6.00$28.90
2Send driver locations less often. A fix every 10 s instead of 5 s (or only after moving ~30 m) halves the Dispatch messages. At large scale, duration drops to about 0.39 M GB-s, inside the included 400,000, which saves the $12.50 duration charge.$0$0.32$14.12
3Answer the trip ping with setWebSocketAutoResponse, or switch to protocol pings. Auto-responses don't wake the object, and protocol pings aren't billed. That takes the pings (about 1.6 M requests a month at large scale) off the bill.$0$0.06$0.24
4Serve images through a cache, not the worker. Put menu images, logos and QR codes on a public R2 bucket with a custom domain and caching, or behind the Cache API. Most Class B reads go away.$0$0$2.88

8Keep queries on indexes. D1 bills every row a query scans. The admin date-range totals scan each store's transactions and are fine at cpanel traffic. Don't move them into the mobile app's hot paths.——— (avoids new cost)
